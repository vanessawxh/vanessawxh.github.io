Flight Cost Analysis with Machine Learning

Year2025
OrganizationE178, Statistics and Data Science
RoleGroup Project
ToolsPython
TagsEngineering · Software

Overview

Flight prices can swing wildly based on timing, class, and booking window, making it hard for budget-conscious travelers (especially students) to plan ahead. For this project, our team built a predictive pricing model using a dataset of 300,000+ flights, engineering features like airline, seat class, stops, and days until departure. We trained and compared 50 models across three approaches. First, a LASSO-regularized linear regression, which fits a straight-line relationship between features and price while automatically zeroing out less useful variables; it reached an R² of 0.908 but struggled to price expensive tickets accurately since it couldn't capture the sharp gap between economy and business fares. Second, a random forest, which builds many decision trees on different slices of the data and averages their predictions, making it naturally suited to handling discrete jumps in price like cabin class; after tuning tree count and depth, our best forest hit an R² of 0.955. Third, a neural network (MLP) built in TensorFlow/Keras, which learns non-linear patterns through layers of interconnected nodes; across 30 architectures, our best designs consistently reached R² ≈ 0.948, picking up on subtle interactions between stops, timing, and airline.

Results

The random forest model (60 estimators, max depth 12) ultimately came out on top, edging out the neural network and outperforming linear regression, which underscored that flight pricing is a fundamentally non-linear problem shaped by discrete pricing tiers rather than smooth trends. The final model offers a practical tool for anticipating price surges and making smarter, more affordable travel decisions.
